Connector guide

Introduce Solvyr when someone owns a document backlog that needs to become AI-ready.

A useful introduction has a workload owner, a bounded backlog or refresh cycle, a target output, and a safe representative sample. European execution context matters when it is attached to that specific run—not as a separate infrastructure pitch.

Good introductions

  • Teams preparing PDFs or text for RAG, search, evals, or agents.
  • Organizations with a recurring corpus refresh or re-indexing need.
  • Public-data teams with metadata exports or catalog quality runs.
  • Archives, libraries, agencies, or AI teams with clean digital document sets.
  • Teams under cost or delivery pressure that can share a safe sample.

Weak introductions

  • Generic curiosity about AI infrastructure without a workload.
  • Highly sensitive data cases without a safe sample.
  • Realtime serving or chatbot hosting needs.
  • Broad platform migration requests.

Suggested intro

Short, concrete, workload-first.

“Solvyr turns clean PDF or text backlogs into inspectable AI-ready corpus output and run evidence through a scoped pilot. Standard clean batches start at EUR 0.95 per 1000 pages. Would this fit one of your current backlogs or refresh cycles?”

After the introduction

Solvyr scopes the run; the connector does not sell the system.

Solvyr handles workload definition, sample boundaries, output acceptance, execution context, and the pilot decision with the owner.
