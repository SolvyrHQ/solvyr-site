About Solvyr

Fast Corpus sells completed corpus work. Controlled distributed execution is the company underneath.

Solvyr is in a founder-led, controlled trial phase. The current public offer is narrow on purpose: clean PDF and text backlogs to AI-ready JSONL, chunks, embeddings, stable IDs, a run report, and a clear starting price for standard batches. For archives and public teams, it also creates a practical way to evaluate European execution control around a real workload.

Vision

Activate underused computers for suitable AI execution and expand useful compute capacity beyond today's data centres.

Mission

Make distributed capacity dependable through controlled, inspectable execution of bounded AI workloads.

Product

A managed execution service: customers buy completed output and run evidence, not raw GPU access or an orchestration project.

Current wedge

Fast Corpus turns clean document and text backlogs into accepted AI-ready corpus output. It is the first workload, not the limit of the company.

Why this shape

A concrete workload is the honest way to prove the system.

Solvyr is not trying to sell infrastructure in the abstract. Fast Corpus gives buyers, connectors, and agents one concrete backlog outcome, output bundle, and cost basis to evaluate before any broader platform or sovereignty conversation.

Company and direction

Founder-led, with one concrete workload as the starting point.

Solvyr is led by founder Jan Wouter van Dalen, who owns product direction, customer development, partnerships, and pilot design. The engineering work is focused on the controlled execution system underneath Fast Corpus. Solvyr OÜ is the operating company and product-development vehicle. Fast Corpus is the first bounded workload through which Solvyr proves dependable asynchronous execution against a result a customer can inspect.

Expansion path

From a completed run to a repeatable system.

Repeated corpus and document workflows exercise the reusable capabilities underneath: scheduling, node eligibility, retries, visible state, result validation, and lifecycle reporting. Europe is the first proving ground for explicit location, ownership, policy, and evidence—not the geographic ceiling of the system.

Intended system outcomes

More useful capacity, control, and scheduling choice.

Better use of existing hardware could support more resilient and plural infrastructure, location-aware execution, and eventually energy-aware scheduling. These outcomes require measurement. Solvyr does not currently claim lower electricity or water use, lower carbon, less data-centre construction, certified sovereignty, or greater efficiency than data-centre GPUs; distributed execution is not automatically more efficient.

Current phase

Controlled trials, representative samples, and capped runs.

A good first conversation starts with document count, page count, input type, desired output, data sensitivity, recurrence, and whether a safe representative sample can be shared.
