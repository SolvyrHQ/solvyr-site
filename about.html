About Solvyr

Fast Corpus sells completed corpus work. Controlled distributed execution is the company underneath.

Solvyr is in a founder-led, controlled trial phase. The current public offer is narrow on purpose: clean PDF and text backlogs to AI-ready JSONL, chunks, embeddings, stable IDs, a run report, and a clear starting price for standard batches. For archives and public teams, it also creates a practical way to evaluate European execution control around a real workload.

What is real now

Controlled async document/text corpus processing, pilot scoping, visible run evidence, predictable starting-unit pricing, and honest fit/non-fit boundaries.

What is being built

A distributed execution system with central scheduling, bounded tasks, retries, durable status, explainable results, and European execution control for workloads that fit.

What is not claimed

No broad self-serve platform, generic GPU rental, enterprise SLA, OCR-heavy support, or sensitive-data processing by default.

Why this shape

A concrete workload is the honest way to prove the system.

Solvyr is not trying to sell infrastructure in the abstract. Fast Corpus gives buyers, connectors, and agents one concrete backlog outcome, output bundle, and cost basis to evaluate before any broader platform or sovereignty conversation.

Company and team

Founder-led, with product and engineering aimed at one proof path.

Solvyr is led by founder Jan Wouter van Dalen, who owns product direction, customer development, partnerships, and pilot design. The engineering work is focused on the scheduler, API, worker execution, and benchmark evidence needed for controlled external runs. Solvyr OÜ is the operating company and product-development vehicle; core Solvyr work and intellectual property are assigned at company level.

Expansion path

From one workload to a reusable execution layer.

Fast Corpus is the first bounded workload through which Solvyr proves dependable asynchronous execution. Each run exercises the same underlying capabilities: accepted workload contracts, task scheduling, node eligibility, retries, visible state, result validation, and lifecycle reporting. The expansion path starts with repeated corpus and document workflows. As those workflows become repeatable, the same controlled execution layer can support additional bounded asynchronous AI workloads without turning Solvyr into a generic compute marketplace.

Research direction

Make trust requirements and execution evidence testable.

Solvyr is investigating how an asynchronous AI workload can declare its trust and execution requirements, and how a scheduler can route it only to eligible execution environments while preserving explainable lifecycle evidence. The research questions include scheduler authority, node eligibility, authorized access, task leases, result acceptance, bounded retries, failure evidence, retention and deletion state, and privacy-conscious observability when infrastructure is operated by another party. The goal is a commercially usable execution system with stronger, testable assurance—not an abstract sovereignty claim. This is an active research and product-development direction; Solvyr does not currently claim formal verification, certification, or support for arbitrary sensitive workloads.

Commercial validation

One external workflow now has a conditional paid-pilot path.

Solvyr is preparing a bounded ERP-adjacent document and data workflow with a commercial validation partner. The partner has recorded its intent to negotiate a paid pilot if technical validation succeeds and the scope, pricing, security, and operating terms are mutually acceptable. This is conditional pilot intent, not a production deployment, purchase order, unconditional payment obligation, or recurring revenue. It gives the development path a real customer workflow, validation criteria, and commercial decision point.

Current phase

Controlled trials, representative samples, and capped runs.

A good first conversation starts with document count, page count, input type, desired output, data sensitivity, recurrence, and whether a safe representative sample can be shared.
